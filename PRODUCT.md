# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Podcast producers and video editors who manage multiple client shows as a service. Solo operators or small teams running production pipelines across several clients simultaneously. They work in high-context environments alongside Frame.io, Premiere Pro, Transistor.fm, and other professional media tools. Their primary job: know where every episode is across every show, move work through the pipeline, and keep clients informed without email back-and-forth.

Secondary users: clients of the producer, who access a simplified portal to check episode status and approve deliverables. Non-technical, low engagement frequency; they want answers at a glance.

## Product Purpose

PreRoll is the orchestration layer for podcast and video production services. It consolidates episode pipelines, client communication, asset organization, and publishing into one place. It doesn't replace specialized tools (Frame.io for review, Transistor for hosting, Canva for design); it connects them and provides the single source of truth for "where is everything."

Success looks like: the producer never has to answer "what's the status of my episode?" because the client can see it themselves.

## Brand Personality

Creative, opinionated, modern.

PreRoll speaks with the confidence of a tool built by someone who actually does this work. It has a point of view about how production should flow. It respects the craft of media production without being precious about it. The tone is direct and assured, never hedging or over-explaining.

## Anti-references

- Playful, cartoonish, or unserious UI. No illustrations of people waving, no rounded-everything whimsy, no pastel toy-like aesthetics.
- Generic SaaS dashboard templates. No hero-metric cards with gradient accents, no identical card grids, no "welcome back!" banners.
- Tools that feel like project management first and media second. PreRoll is media-native, not Trello with a podcast skin.

## Design Principles

1. **Media-native, not PM-generic.** Every screen should feel like it was built for someone who edits video and ships episodes, not someone managing sprints. Vocabulary, layout, and density should reflect production workflows.
2. **Status at a glance.** The producer manages many shows. The most important information (what needs attention, what's blocked, what's next) should be visible without clicking into anything.
3. **Confident defaults.** The tool should be opinionated about workflow (Planning → Recording → Editing → Review → Approved → Published) rather than offering infinite customization. Strong defaults, escape hatches when needed.
4. **Respect the client's time.** The portal is not the product; it's a window into the product. Clients should get their answer in under 10 seconds. No learning curve, no onboarding tour, no feature discovery.
5. **Professional gravity.** The interface should feel like a tool that serious producers rely on, not a startup experiment. Weight and density over airiness and whitespace.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reasonable defaults: WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratios, keyboard navigable, no motion-dependent interactions, respects prefers-reduced-motion. No specific accommodations beyond standard best practices.
